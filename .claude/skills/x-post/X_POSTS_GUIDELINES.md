# X (Twitter) Post Writing Guidelines

Rules for writing posts for **Tenderwize** — a SaaS platform that helps companies find, analyze and win public procurement tenders (EU TED data, AI tender matching, deep bid/no-bid analysis).

These rules are the source of truth for any content generated by the admin "X post writer" feature, and for the `x-post` skill. Every generated post should be checkable against this file.

## 1. Audience & goal

- Audience: founders/BD/sales people at SMEs and contractors who bid on public tenders (construction, IT, healthcare, consulting, supplies), plus procurement/GovTech-curious followers.
- Goal of every post: earn a save/reply/repost from that audience, or drive a click to the product — never just "announce something."
- One post = one idea. If you need two ideas, write two posts or a thread.

## 2. Voice & tone

- Direct, concrete, slightly opinionated. Sounds like a practitioner who reads tenders for a living, not a marketing department.
- No corporate fluff: banned words/phrases — "revolutionize", "game-changer", "unlock", "seamless", "in today's fast-paced world", "we are excited to announce".
- Confident, not hypey. Back claims with a number, an example, or a screenshot — never an unqualified superlative ("the best", "the only").
- First person plural ("we") only when speaking about the product/team. Second person ("you") when giving advice to the reader.
- Humor is fine if it's dry/observational; never at the expense of a specific buyer, contracting authority, or competitor.

## 3. Content pillars

There are exactly five post types. Rotate across them — don't post the same type twice in a row. Every generated/drafted post must declare which of the five it is.

For Interesting Tender, Market Insight, and Contract Award, the admin AI generator fetches the real facts itself — it queries Tenderwize's tender/award database (submission deadlines, buyers, winners, amounts, counts) and is only allowed to cite what a query actually returned; if nothing matches, it refuses to generate rather than inventing data. Historical Story grounds itself the same way but against the open web instead of Tenderwize's database, since the events it covers predate or fall outside TED coverage (see its section below). Procurement Insight needs no query. When drafting by hand instead (e.g. via the `x-post` skill without the generator), apply the same discipline yourself: look up the real record or stat before writing, never approximate one.

### Value first, then product

Every pillar pairs a piece of real procurement value with a soft demonstration of what Tenderwize can do — never the other way around. The formula:

```
Procurement value → Tenderwize capability → relevant CTA/link
```

Lead with the fact, problem, or insight. Only once that's established, and only if it fits naturally, connect it to the relevant Tenderwize capability. Never open with the product.

Not this:

```
Tenderwize has powerful tender filters...
```

But this:

```
Searching only by keywords can cause relevant tenders to be missed.
...
Tenderwize lets you combine keywords, categories and other filters...
```

The product half of the formula is optional, not mandatory — don't force a feature mention into every single post. If the connection would feel forced, it's enough to end with a relevant link (section 9) and skip the feature callout entirely. A post that's 100% value with no product mention is a valid outcome, not a miss.

When you do name a capability, pick it from `FEATURES.md` (same skill folder) only. It's the fixed list of what Tenderwize actually does — never invent, guess, or embellish a feature that isn't on that list, the same discipline as the no-fabricated-URLs rule in section 9.

**The CTA/link step stops being optional once you use the capability step.** A feature mention only counts as a real CTA if the reader can click through on it — "Tenderwize's tender analysis surfaces key requirements first" with no URL after it is not a complete formula, it's a dangling product mention. So:

- Named a feature? → the post must end with a link (homepage at minimum, or a more specific page per section 9's priority if one was supplied).
- Not naming a feature? → the link stays fully optional, per each pillar's own rule in section 9.

Don't work around this by naming a feature and then skipping the link because "it didn't feel natural" — if the link doesn't fit, drop the feature mention instead and stay pure value-only.

### 🔥 Interesting Tender — Opportunity + Discovery

**Goal:** surface one specific, currently open tender and unobtrusively demonstrate Tenderwize as the way it was found: show the opportunity, lead with the key facts, then drive a click to its Tenderwize page.

- What qualifies: a large estimated value, a notable/well-known buyer, an unusual procurement subject, or just an unusually good fit for the audience (generous deadline, low competition category, etc.).
- **Must be currently open** — check the submission deadline is still in the future before posting. Never present an expired tender as a live opportunity.
- Ground it in real fields: buyer name/country, title/subject, CPV category, estimated value, submission deadline. Never invent or round up a number you don't have.
- Structure: hook = the single most striking fact (amount, buyer, or subject) in one line; 1-2 lines on why it's worth a look; CTA = link to the tender's Tenderwize page, ideally in the first reply — see section 9 for the link rules.
- This data is public (TED notices are public by nature), so citing/linking the real notice is fine.

#### Pattern detection: why is it interesting, not just how big

**"€20bn" is not a story on its own — why the number is €20bn is.** The old approach (sort currently-open tenders by value_desc, hand the top one to the model) treats "biggest number" as the reason to post, and produces exactly the failure this section exists to prevent: a Dynamic Purchasing System's €20bn *ceiling* over several years gets phrased as if Italy is buying €20bn of medicines outright, and the supporting line becomes AI filler ("a major opportunity... across Italy") that isn't actually backed by anything in the data.

Interesting Tender selection now runs the same detect-first pipeline as Company Story:

1. **DB pattern detection (deterministic, no LLM)** — `interesting-tender-detector.ts` scores a candidate pool of currently-open tenders (the highest-value ones, unioned with the soonest-closing ones above €500k so a short deadline isn't starved by a value-only sort) against real baselines, and keeps the best-scoring candidate if it clears `INTERESTING_TENDER_REQUIRED_SCORE` (0.6). If nothing clears the bar, generation falls back to the previous tool-based pick (still correctly hedged on DPS/framework values, see below) rather than refusing outright — unlike Company Story, Interesting Tender's job is to always surface *some* current opportunity.
2. **Web enrichment (optional)** — only for `exceptional_value` and `long_term_mechanism` patterns, where getting the value's meaning right matters most: `WebEnrichmentService.enrichInterestingTender` searches for the buyer + tender/mechanism name against the same allowlist as Company Story. A miss here doesn't disqualify the tender (the DB facts are already correctly framed either way) — it just means the post runs on Tenderwize data alone instead of with added real-world context.
3. **LLM phrasing** — the model receives the confirmed `detectedStory` and is only asked to phrase it, exactly like Company Story.

| Pattern | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| **Exceptional value** | Estimated value ≥5x the median of ≥5 other currently-open tenders in the same CPV division (never just "the biggest we found") |
| **Long-term mechanism** | A DPS or framework agreement running ≥2 years |
| **Complex structure** | ≥5 lots |
| **Short deadline** | ≤10 days remaining AND estimated value ≥€500k (never a trivial tender that's simply about to close) |
| **Cross-border** | Performance spans ≥2 countries |
| **Rare category** | ≤3 tenders (any status, all time) share this tender's exact leading CPV code |
| **Unusual buyer** | The buyer has published ≤2 tenders in our data AND this one is ≥€1M |

**Critical value-framing rule.** Before stating any amount, check whether the tender is a Dynamic Purchasing System or framework agreement (`isDps`/`isFrameworkAgreement`, surfaced on every open-tender record). If either is true, the estimated value is a **ceiling/indicative maximum spread over the mechanism's whole lifetime — never a single purchase**.

- Wrong: *"€20bn for medicines."* (implies one purchase)
- Right: *"A €20bn ceiling for a pharmaceutical purchasing system."* / *"ESTAR has set up a dynamic purchasing system for medicines, with an indicated value of up to €20bn running to [deadline]."*

This rule applies universally — whether the tender was selected via the new pattern detector or the older tool-based fallback.

**The notes field must say why, not restate provenance.** `notes` should name the specific pattern and its number (e.g. *"exceptional_value: ~12x the category median"*), never a generic line like *"a major opportunity"* or a data-provenance restatement like *"currently open; deadline, buyer, value confirmed"* — the latter tells the reader nothing they can't already see in the post itself, and reads as filler in the admin UI.

### 📊 Market Insight — Data + Analytics

**Goal:** turn Tenderwize's own aggregated data into procurement intelligence — stats and trends across countries, industries, tender counts, values, and momentum — while the post itself demonstrates the platform's value as a procurement-intelligence source.

- What qualifies: how many tenders/awards appeared over a period, which countries or industries (CPV categories) are trending up or down, total procurement volume, period-over-period comparisons.
- Always state the time window and that it's Tenderwize-tracked data (e.g. "across the N tenders Tenderwize tracked in Q3" or "vs. the same period last year") — never present a stat as if it's the entire EU market unless it demonstrably is.
- Observation, not causation: state what the data shows, not why it's happening, unless the cause is independently verifiable.
- Structure: hook = the number/trend itself; 1-2 lines of context on why it matters to a bidder; CTA is optional and usually skippable — this pillar's job is credibility, not clicks.

#### Pattern detection: a scanned trend, not "explore and report whatever you find"

The old approach let the model "explore the data yourself" across metrics/countries/categories with a few tool calls and report "whichever trend is the most interesting" — the same failure mode as sorting by value_desc, just for trends: nothing stopped the model from calling "2 tenders became 3" a 50% surge.

Market Insight now runs the same detect-first pipeline as the other pillars, via `market-insight-detector.ts`:

1. **DB pattern detection (deterministic, no LLM)** — `XPostDataService.findMarketInsightSlices` computes a current-vs-previous-period (90 days by default) comparison for "Overall" plus the top 10 most active countries and top 10 most active CPV divisions by recent volume. Each slice is scored and the backend keeps only the best one if it clears `MARKET_INSIGHT_REQUIRED_SCORE` (0.6). If nothing clears the bar, generation falls back to the previous tool-based exploration.
2. **LLM phrasing** — the model receives the confirmed trend and only phrases it, exactly like the other pillars.

| Pattern | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| **Surge / decline** | ≥5 tenders in the current period AND ≥40% change vs. the previous period (never a swing between two tiny counts) |
| **New activity** | ≥5 tenders in the current period, essentially none (≤1) in the previous one |

Detection deliberately runs on **tender counts only**, never on the value-sum metric — a single outlier tender can distort a monetary total by orders of magnitude (the exact "one big number" problem this whole detect-first approach exists to avoid), so count is the safer trend signal. When a slice's disclosed value total is available, it's added as supporting scale ("worth a combined €340M") — never as the trigger for the pattern itself.

`notes` must name the specific trend, scope, and magnitude (e.g. *"surge: +85% tenders in Germany's CPV 45 category over 90 days"*) — never a generic reason.

### 🏆 Contract Award — Award + Market Intelligence

**Goal:** show the money moving — who won, from whom, what was procured, and for how much — and connect it to Tenderwize's ability to research awards, suppliers, and procurement history.

- Ground it in real award fields: buyer (`leadBuyers`/`nonLeadBuyers`), winner (`winners`), awarded value (`awardValue`/`awardValueEur`), what was procured (title/CPV codes), and the award/conclusion date.
- Structure: hook = buyer + winner + amount in a single line; 1 line on what was actually procured; optional context (e.g. how it compares to the original estimated value) if it adds a real insight.
- This data is public by definition (TED result notices) — cite it plainly. Don't speculate about the winning bidder's strategy or pricing beyond what the notice discloses.
- **Value-framing rule (applies to every Contract Award post, not just Company Story):** check `isDps`/`isFrameworkAgreement` on the award's lots before stating the amount. If either is true, `awardValueEur` is a ceiling/indicative maximum over the mechanism's whole lifetime — **never phrase it as a lump sum paid to the winner**. Same rule, same wording pattern as Interesting Tender's DPS/framework fix (see that pillar's section) — e.g. "a €2.66bn framework ceiling", not "awarded Correos a €2.66bn framework".
- **Don't just pick the highest-value recent award and describe it** — that's a "biggest number" post, not a reason to care. Look for what's actually notable about the record before writing (unusually large relative to typical awards in the category, a rare buyer, a striking detail) — and say what that reason is in `notes` (see the wording rule under "Pattern confidence ≠ storyworthiness" below): never a bare data-provenance line like "selected the highest-value recent award returned".

#### Story angles

A pillar is *what* the post is about; a **story angle** is *how* it's told within that pillar — a second level under a pillar, not a pillar of its own. Contract Award currently supports:

- **Auto** (default) — the backend looks for a Company Story candidate first (see below) and falls back to a single fresh award pick if none qualifies.
- **Company Story** — instead of one fresh award announcement, the post is built around a supplier's award history already in Tenderwize's data: growth from an early/smallest award to the latest/largest, a repeat relationship with the same buyer, a new record-largest award, or expansion into a new buyer/country.

**Company Story is a three-stage pipeline, and the LLM only ever runs the last stage.**

```
Tenderwize DB → pattern detection → web enrichment → verified external facts → LLM writes the post
```

1. **DB pattern detection (deterministic, no LLM)** — `XPostDataService.buildCompanyStoryFacts` fetches every award naming a supplier and runs the full history through `company-story-detector.ts`, which scores eight specific, computable patterns (below) and keeps only the best one *if* it scores at least `COMPANY_STORY_REQUIRED_SCORE` (0.70). A candidate with no pattern above that bar is dropped immediately — the backend scans up to 50 suppliers (`COMPANY_STORY_SCAN_LIMIT`), ranks every one with a confirmed pattern by score, and keeps the top 10 (`COMPANY_STORY_RANK_KEEP`), since detection itself is pure arithmetic and cheap.
2. **Web enrichment (deterministic search + a strict extraction call, no story-writing LLM yet)** — `WebEnrichmentService` tries the top-ranked candidates in score order (capped at 3, `COMPANY_STORY_ENRICH_ATTEMPTS`, since this stage costs real search-API + fetch + LLM-extraction calls) and searches Brave Search for the specific award/buyer pairing the pattern is anchored on — not "tell me about this company" but `"<award title>" "<buyer>"`, `"<supplier>" "<buyer>" contract`, `"<supplier>" "<award title>"`. Results are filtered to an allowlist (below) before anything is fetched, and a separate, narrower LLM call (`extractExternalFacts`) extracts only facts that are literally present in the fetched page text — it cannot add outside knowledge, and every returned fact's `sourceUrl` is verified server-side against the pages actually fetched before being trusted.
3. **LLM phrasing** — the model receives the confirmed `detectedStory` (type, score, summary, evidence) plus, when found, the verified `externalFacts` (each with its own `sourceUrl`), and is only asked to phrase them into a post. It's explicitly told not to search for a different pattern, not to second-guess the DB detection, and never to state anything beyond what's in `detectedStory.evidence` or an `externalFacts[].fact`.

**If web enrichment finds nothing usable for a candidate, that candidate is treated as if it had no story at all** — the pipeline moves to the next-ranked candidate rather than writing from the DB pattern alone. (This only applies when `BRAVE_API_KEY` is configured; with it unset, web enrichment is disabled entirely and Company Story runs on DB detection alone, exactly as before this stage existed.)

This split exists because leaving pattern-finding to the model produces false stories: given `€800,000 award → €3,625 award`, a model will readily narrate it as "a shift" or "decline" even though a supplier can simply win contracts of any size in any order — a two-point contrast has no interpretable meaning on its own. Web enrichment exists because even a *real* DB pattern (e.g. Amgen's 64/65 pharmaceutical awards) is often a dry statistic on its own — a press release or official page about the actual project can turn "64 out of 65" into a sentence about what the money is actually for.

#### Web enrichment sourcing rules

- **Allowed sources only**: the supplier's own official site (matched heuristically against the supplier's name — never a hardcoded per-company domain list, since this has to work for any supplier globally), any `.gov`/`.gob`/`.gouv`/`europa.eu` domain, or a small curated list of reputable business press/wire services (Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ, Forbes, BusinessWire, PRNewswire, GlobeNewswire, Politico Europe, Euractiv, MarketWatch, CNBC, The Guardian, The Economist, TechCrunch — see `TRUSTED_MEDIA_DOMAINS` in `web-enrichment.service.ts`). Everything else (blogs, aggregators, social media, unknown sites) is rejected before a page is even fetched.
- **Every fact is stored with its source**: `{ fact, sourceUrl, sourceTitle, publishedAt, retrievedAt }` — no web-derived fact is ever folded into a post's context without this record. Drafts show "Story source: Tenderwize data + N external sources" and list each fact with a clickable source link, so it can be checked before publishing.
- **External sources never become the post's link** — the Tenderwize award page stays the CTA; a source URL from enrichment is for factual grounding and admin verification only, never a competing outbound link (see section 9).

#### Meaningful Narrative Rule

**A numerical difference alone is NOT a story.** None of the following are sufficient by themselves:

- largest award vs. latest award
- first award vs. latest award
- one large award followed by one small award (or vice versa)
- award count increasing by one
- two awards with different buyers
- two awards with different values

Never imply a supplier "shifted", "grew", "declined", "expanded", "moved", or "changed strategy" based only on a chronological difference between two individual awards. A Company Story requires a pattern supported by **multiple observations** (a trend across periods, a relationship repeated several times) or a genuinely **exceptional event** measured against a real baseline (a median, a round threshold) — never a bare two-point comparison.

#### Valid Company Story patterns

Each pattern has a minimum evidence bar *before* it's even scored — these aren't stylistic preferences, they're implemented as hard gates in `company-story-detector.ts`:

| Pattern | Minimum evidence | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth** | ≥3 distinct calendar years with awards, non-decreasing count or value across all of them, ≥2x from first to last | 2023: 2 awards/€400k → 2024: 5/€1.8M → 2025: 11/€6.2M |
| **Acceleration** | ≥5 dated awards, second half of the timeline has ≥2x and ≥3 more awards than the first half | 3 awards in 2023-2024 vs. 12 in 2025-2026 |
| **Repeat Buyer** | Same buyer on ≥3 awards | 5 awards from the same contracting authority over 3 years |
| **Buyer Expansion** | ≤2 buyers in the first half of the timeline, ≥3 genuinely new buyers in the second half | Started with 1 buyer, now also winning from 4 others |
| **Market Expansion** | ≤1 country in the first half, ≥1 new country in the second half | Domestic-only history, then a first award abroad |
| **Exceptional Win** | ≥5 valued awards, top award ≥3x the **median of its other awards** (never just the previous one) | Median award €180k; new award €4.8M (26x) |
| **Specialization** | ≥6 awards with a known CPV code, ≥60% share the same CPV division, AND enough reach across buyers/countries/years to make the concentration informative rather than just a small niche player's whole business — see below | 13 of 16 awards are road maintenance work |
| **Milestone** | The award count itself is a round number (25, 50, 100, …), or the latest award pushed cumulative disclosed value past a round threshold (€10M, €25M, €50M, …) | The 50th recorded award; cumulative value just crossed €100M |

Growth is never inferred from two individual awards — it requires the multi-year buckets above. A milestone is never manufactured from an arbitrary number — only a genuinely round one, and (for the value case) only when the *latest* award is the one that crossed it.

Each pattern gets a 0-1 score from its own formula (strength of the trend, size of the ratio, count of repeats, etc.); the backend keeps the single best-scoring pattern for that supplier and requires it to be ≥0.70 to qualify at all:

```
Company: REO Investment
  growth             0.12
  acceleration       0.08
  repeat_buyer       0.21
  buyer_expansion    0.14
  exceptional_win    0.34
  specialization     0.28
  milestone          0.05
Best score: 0.34 — required: 0.70 → NO_STORY_FOUND, try the next company
```

**The one-sentence test.** The model's `notes` field must answer *"Why is this interesting?"* by referencing the specific confirmed pattern (e.g. "acceleration: 3 awards in the first half of its history vs. 12 in the second") — never a generic reason like "it has several awards" or "the total is large".

**If no supplier among the scanned candidates clears the 0.70 bar — or, with web enrichment enabled, if none of the top-ranked candidates can be corroborated on the open web — no Company Story is generated.** This is expected and correct — better to scan 50 companies and find nothing than turn a random €800k-then-€3,625 pairing into a manufactured "story". Auto then falls back to a plain award pick; explicitly requesting Company Story fails with a clear "no strong company story found" message instead of forcing one.

#### Pattern confidence ≠ storyworthiness

A mathematically strong pattern isn't automatically an interesting one. A pharmaceutical company having 64 of 65 awards in the pharmaceutical category is a very strong Specialization signal (ratio ≈0.98) — and completely unsurprising, since that's presumably most of what the company does. It's not wrong, it's just not a *story*.

We deliberately don't ask the model to judge this by reasoning about what kind of company the supplier "is" (e.g. inferring "Amgen is a pharma company" from its name) — that's world knowledge the model would have to guess at, which is exactly the kind of hallucination risk this whole pipeline exists to avoid. Instead, Specialization scores two things separately, both from data already in hand:

- **Pattern confidence** — the concentration ratio itself (e.g. 0.98 for 64/65).
- **Storyworthiness** — whether that concentration holds up across enough distinct buyers, countries, and years to be informative rather than an artifact of the supplier simply being small and narrow in every dimension. A supplier with one buyer in one country trivially looks "specialized" — that's not a finding. A supplier winning from many different buyers across multiple countries and years that *still* concentrates on one category is the genuinely tellable case.

Both must clear their own bar (see `detectSpecialization` in `company-story-detector.ts`) before Specialization is even eligible to compete with the other seven patterns — a high ratio alone is not enough. Do not generate a Company Story solely because category concentration is high.

#### Phrasing quality: lead with the number, not the label

Even a confirmed pattern can still read flat if the post states the *label* instead of the *fact*. "That's a clear specialization." tells the reader nothing they can picture — it restates the pattern's name.

- Weak: *"Amgen shows a clear specialization in its public procurement footprint."*
- Better: *"64 out of 65. That's how concentrated Amgen's disclosed awards are in one category — pharmaceutical products, per Tenderwize data."*

Lead with the concrete number/ratio/contrast from the evidence, then explain what it means in plain language. When `knownTotalValueEur` is available, add it as a second, separate fact (scale on top of the pattern) rather than folding it into the hook — e.g. pattern first, then "Its disclosed awards in our data total €386.8M." as a supporting line.

**Wording rule (critical):** Tenderwize's data is a partial view of any company's real contract history — never phrase a Company Story as the company's total or complete record.

- Wrong: "Company X has won 7 public contracts." / "has earned €28.4M from public procurement."
- Right: "Company X appears in 7 awards in Tenderwize's data." / "we found 7 awards" / "its disclosed awards in our data total €28.4M."

Never sum award values across different currencies — `awardValueEur` is already EUR-normalized for every award, so summing that field is safe; never pair it with a record's original (possibly non-EUR) `awardValue.currency`.

**Repetition control:** the same supplier isn't reselected for a Company Story within 90 days at all, and not again before 365 days unless something meaningfully changed since the last story (a different confirmed pattern type, a materially bigger record award, a materially bigger disclosed total, or a new buyer/country appearing) — see `hasMeaningfulDevelopment` in `x-post.service.ts`.

### 💡 Procurement Insight — Education + Product

**Goal:** a useful procurement tip paired with the relevant Tenderwize feature that addresses it — no live data required, evergreen.

- Topics: CPV codes, framework agreements, how to search/read tenders effectively, how award criteria weighting works, common reasons bids get disqualified, quirks and gotchas of public procurement.
- One tactical tip per post, with a concrete example — never a generic platitude ("communication is key").
- Follow the value-first formula: state the problem/insight first, then (if it fits naturally) the Tenderwize feature that solves it. Examples of the pairing:
  - Problem: keyword-only search misses relevant tenders → Feature: CPV/category search.
  - Problem: hard to keep track of new tenders as they're published → Feature: alerts.
  - Problem: procurement is spread across many countries and sources → Feature: unified cross-country search.
- Product mentions are optional and must stay soft — this pillar's job is to teach first, pitch second. If the tip doesn't have a natural product tie-in, don't manufacture one; a relevant link is enough. But if you do name a feature, the post must end with a link to it (or the homepage) — see section 9. Never name a feature and leave it as a dangling, unlinked mention.

### 📜 Historical Story — Editorial + Context

**Goal:** a real, verifiable historical business/procurement story (a famous cost overrun, a landmark contract, a procurement scandal) told well — evergreen and editorial like Procurement Insight, but narrative rather than tactical.

**This pillar has no Tenderwize DB signal at all** — the events it covers (the Sydney Opera House, the Big Dig, Apollo-program contracts, and similar) predate or fall outside TED coverage. Every other pillar grounds itself in Tenderwize's own database; this one instead grounds itself entirely in **web-enrichment-verified external facts** (`WebEnrichmentService.enrichHistoricalStory`), reusing the same allow-listed search/fetch/extract pipeline as Company Story and Interesting Tender — widened here to include reputable encyclopedic sources (Wikipedia, Britannica, national archives, major newspapers) appropriate for well-established history, which wouldn't be trusted for a live company/tender claim elsewhere in this system.

- **Topic selection:** the backend rotates through a curated seed list (`historical-story-topics.ts` — real, well-documented events only, reviewed and append-only) avoiding whatever this pillar posted most recently, resetting once the list has fully cycled. Up to 5 candidate topics are tried per generation; a topic the web can't corroborate at all is skipped for the next one, same discipline as Company Story's "no story found, try the next candidate."
- **This pillar requires `BRAVE_API_KEY`.** With it unset, generation refuses outright rather than letting the model narrate history from its own training-data memory — there is nothing else to ground it in, and an unverified "historical fact" from an LLM is exactly the hallucination risk this whole detect-first architecture exists to prevent.
- **Every specific claim (a date, an amount, a name, an outcome, a quote) MUST come from the verified `externalFacts`** — never from what the model already "knows" about the topic, even when it's confident and even when the fact is genuinely true, because it can't be verified the way everything else here is. If enrichment turns up too little to support a real narrative, the generator refuses (`NO_STORY_FOUND`) rather than filling the gap from memory.
- Structure: hook = the single most striking verified fact in one line; 2-4 lines telling the actual story in chronological/cause-effect order, using only verified facts; optionally one line drawing a soft, honest parallel to procurement today — never claim Tenderwize could have "prevented" the historical event, that's not a verifiable claim.
- Don't phrase this as a live Tenderwize discovery ("Tenderwize data shows...") — it's public history, not something Tenderwize tracked or found.
- No Tenderwize record exists to link to. Per section 9, use the homepage only if a natural tie-in fits, otherwise no link — and never link to one of the `externalFacts` source URLs as the post's own CTA (those are for factual grounding and admin verification, exactly like every other pillar's external sources).

## 4. Card image

Every post gets an auto-generated card image (1200×675) alongside the text — a dark branded card with the pillar name and accent color, the post's text as the headline, and the `tenderwize.com` mark. It's rendered from the first post in `posts[]`, so **the hook must stand on its own** (see section 5).

The renderer automatically strips any link/CTA line before drawing the headline — a URL is never clickable on a static image, so it would just be dead text taking up space. Don't rely on this as a substitute for writing a real link in the post itself (section 9); it only prevents an unusable URL from being burned into the image.

Each pillar has a fixed accent color so the five types stay visually distinguishable at a glance: 🔥 Interesting Tender = orange, 📊 Market Insight = blue, 🏆 Contract Award = gold, 💡 Procurement Insight = purple, 📜 Historical Story = teal. Don't change these per post.

## 5. Formats

- **Single post**: one self-contained idea. Default format — use unless the idea genuinely needs more than ~280 characters of setup.
- **Thread**: only when the idea has real sequential steps or evidence (a walkthrough, a list with explanation per item, a case study). Never pad a single-post idea into a thread to look more substantial.
- **Reply/quote**: commentary on tender/procurement/GovTech conversations already happening — add a specific fact or counterpoint, don't just agree.
- **Poll**: sparingly, only when the answer is genuinely useful to know (e.g. "what blocks you most from bidding on more tenders?").

## 6. Structure of a single post

1. **Hook (line 1)** — must stand alone and create curiosity or state the payoff. This is the only part visible before "Show more" on longer posts, and it's what decides if anyone reads on. Lead with a number, a surprising fact, a question, or a bold claim — never with "I want to share..." or "Here's a thread on...".
2. **Body (2-5 short lines/paragraphs)** — one supporting point per line. Use line breaks generously; no walls of text. Concrete > abstract: names, numbers, dates, CPV codes, € amounts beat generic statements.
3. **Payoff/CTA (last line, optional)** — the takeaway, a question to prompt replies, or a soft CTA. Never stack more than one CTA.

Target length: 1-3 short lines is often enough. Hard cap is the platform limit (280 chars for a standard post), but shorter and punchier beats using the full limit.

## 7. Thread structure (when used)

- Post 1 = the hook alone, written so it works as a standalone post too (this is what gets seen/reposted).
- Each subsequent post = exactly one point/step. Don't merge two ideas into one thread post.
- Number posts only if it aids scanning (e.g. "1/", "2/") and the thread has 4+ parts; skip numbering for short threads.
- Close with a recap or a single clear CTA — not both a recap and a CTA stacked.

## 8. Formatting rules

- **Emojis**: 0-2 per post, used as visual markers (bullets, emphasis), never decoratively at the end of every line. When in doubt, leave them out.
- **Hashtags**: max 1, only if it's a real, actively-used tag (e.g. `#PublicProcurement`); never stack 3-5 generic hashtags — it reads as spam and doesn't help reach on this platform.
- **Links**: a post with an external link gets less algorithmic reach. Either accept that tradeoff deliberately for a real destination (blog post, tender notice), or drop the link into the first reply instead of the main post. See section 9 for where the link/URL itself is allowed to come from.
- **Capitalization**: sentence case. No ALL CAPS for emphasis — use word choice or line breaks instead.
- **Line breaks**: prefer short lines over long run-on sentences; a post that reads well broken into 3-4 lines is better than the same content as one paragraph.

## 9. Links and CTA

Every generated post SHOULD include a relevant Tenderwize URL when the post has a useful destination.

The URL MUST come from one of exactly two sources:

1. A tool-call record's own `url` field, used verbatim — never modified.
2. A URL built from **ROUTES.md** — the frontend's live route manifest, fetched fresh from `{CLIENT_URL}/ROUTES.md` on every generation — by taking one of its exact path patterns and substituting any placeholder with a real value that a tool result actually returned earlier in the same generation (an id, country code, CPV category, etc.).

The AI MUST NEVER invent, guess, or construct a URL any other way: no path/pattern that isn't listed in ROUTES.md for that generation, no placeholder value that wasn't actually returned by a query, no domain other than the one ROUTES.md was fetched from. Even a correctly-shaped guess counts as fabrication if it wasn't built this way. If ROUTES.md couldn't be fetched for this generation (network failure, `CLIENT_URL` not configured), treat it as unavailable and fall back per the priority list below — never reuse a path pattern from memory of a previous generation.

As a backstop, the backend strips any link whose domain doesn't match a URL it actually supplied for that generation before a post is saved — so a fabricated domain gets silently dropped rather than published, but don't rely on that; get it right in the draft.

When drafting by hand via the `x-post` skill (no live generator/tool-calling), apply the same rule: fetch or ask for the current `ROUTES.md` content before proposing a link, and build the URL only from a pattern actually in it plus a real value you looked up — never a remembered or guessed path.

### What's actually available today

- **Interesting Tender**: `search_open_tenders` records already carry a real `tenderwize.com/en/tenders/{id}` URL (see `x-post-data.service.ts`) — use it as returned, don't rebuild it from ROUTES.md.
- **Contract Award**: `search_contract_awards` does not return a page URL directly. Look for a matching award/contract page pattern in ROUTES.md and build it from the award's real id; if no such pattern exists, fall back per the priority list below — don't fabricate one from the award id.
- **Market Insight**: no per-record URL comes back from the stats tool. Look for a matching country/industry landing-page pattern in ROUTES.md, using the real country/CPV values from the query that was actually run; otherwise fall back to the homepage or skip the link.
- **Procurement Insight**: no query is made at all. Check ROUTES.md for a landing page relevant to the topic; otherwise fall back to the homepage.
- **Historical Story**: no Tenderwize record exists for a historical event. Use the homepage only if a natural tie-in fits, otherwise skip the link — never link to one of the enrichment's own source URLs as the post's CTA.

### Link priority

Always link to the most specific relevant page actually buildable from what you have.

Priority:

1. A URL returned directly in a tool result's own `url` field.
2. A URL built from a ROUTES.md pattern + real values from tool results (specific tender/award/country/industry page).
3. The Tenderwize homepage URL supplied for this generation.
4. No link at all.

Do not link to the homepage if a more relevant destination is actually buildable from ROUTES.md.

### Rules by pillar

**Interesting Tender** — always link directly to the tender.

Preferred CTA:

```
Explore the tender ↓
{url}
```

or:

```
View the opportunity ↓
{url}
```

**Contract Award** — link directly to the contract/award page when ROUTES.md has a matching pattern (see above). Don't invent a substitute link just to have a CTA.

Preferred CTA:

```
View the contract ↓
{url}
```

or:

```
See the award ↓
{url}
```

**Market Insight** — link to the page that best matches the data being discussed, only if that URL was actually supplied.

Example — post: "Germany published 384 new IT tenders..." → preferred destination: Germany × IT Services page.

```
Explore IT tenders in Germany ↓
{url}
```

**Procurement Insight** — a link is optional only if the post stays pure value with no product mention. The moment the post names a specific Tenderwize feature (section 3), the link becomes mandatory: link to a relevant landing page if one was supplied, otherwise the homepage.

Preferred CTA:

```
Explore European tenders ↓
{url}
```

Don't force a CTA if it makes the post feel unnatural.

**Historical Story** — no record exists to link to; a link is optional and usually skippable. If a soft, honest parallel to procurement today naturally leads into a Tenderwize mention, the homepage is the only allowed destination — never one of the enrichment's own source URLs.

### CTA style

Keep CTAs short and informational.

Good: "Explore the tender ↓", "View the contract ↓", "Explore IT tenders in Germany ↓", "See more opportunities ↓", "Explore European tenders ↓".

Avoid: "Click here", "Learn more now", "Don't miss out", "Sign up today", "Start winning tenders", "Check it out".

### Important

The CTA and URL count toward the post's character limit (section 6) — the final post must stay within it.

Never output placeholders such as `{url}`, `[link]`, `INSERT_URL`, or a hand-typed `tenderwize.com/...` guess in the published text — those are draft-time tokens only, and must be replaced with a real URL from context before the post is finalized.

If no valid URL is available for the destination you want, either omit the link entirely or use the explicitly-provided Tenderwize homepage URL. Never fabricate a destination.

## 10. Do's and don'ts

**Do:**
- Lead with the most interesting/specific fact you have.
- Follow the value-first formula from section 3: procurement value → Tenderwize capability (optional) → relevant CTA/link.
- Cite real numbers from real (public) tender/award data for Interesting Tender, Market Insight, and Contract Award posts.
- Write the way you'd explain it to one specific person, not "an audience".
- Read the post out loud before publishing — if it sounds like a press release, rewrite it.

**Don't:**
- Don't publish unverified claims about win rates, savings, or customer results.
- Don't name a contracting authority or bidder in a way that implies non-public information — only reference what's in the public TED notice.
- Don't post an Interesting Tender whose submission deadline has already passed.
- Don't use engagement-bait phrasing ("this will blow your mind", "you won't believe").
- Don't post two pieces of content back to back that make the same point.
- Don't end every post with a link and "Learn more" — vary the CTA, and often have no CTA at all.
- Don't construct a URL yourself except by following an exact pattern from ROUTES.md with real values that actually came back from a query — never a path that isn't in ROUTES.md, and never a placeholder value you made up (see section 9).
- Don't open a post with the product ("Tenderwize has powerful filters...") — the fact or problem always comes first, the feature (if any) second.
- Don't reference a Tenderwize feature that isn't in `FEATURES.md` — treat an unlisted capability the same as an unverified data claim.
- Don't name a specific feature and then leave it without a link — if a link genuinely doesn't fit, drop the feature mention instead (section 3).

## 11. Compliance & brand safety

- Only reference tender/award data that is genuinely public (TED notices are public by nature — fine to cite/link them directly).
- Never publish customer names, logos, or specific results without their explicit approval on file.
- No unverifiable superlatives ("#1", "the only tool that...") without a citable source.
- No political commentary beyond factual procurement-policy news.

## 12. Pre-publish checklist

Before a post is scheduled/published, it should pass all of these:

- [ ] One idea, not two.
- [ ] Value/fact comes before any product mention, not after (section 3) — and the product mention isn't forced in if the connection feels unnatural.
- [ ] Any named Tenderwize feature is actually on the list in `FEATURES.md` — not invented or embellished.
- [ ] If a feature is named, the post ends with a real link (homepage minimum) — no dangling, unlinked feature mentions.
- [ ] It declares which of the five pillars it is (Interesting Tender / Market Insight / Contract Award / Procurement Insight / Historical Story).
- [ ] First line works as a standalone hook — it's also what renders on the card image (section 4).
- [ ] No banned buzzwords (see section 2).
- [ ] Under the character limit with room to spare, unless it's a deliberate thread.
- [ ] Any claim (number, result, quote) is sourced or approved.
- [ ] For Interesting Tender: the submission deadline is verified still in the future.
- [ ] For Market Insight: the time window and data source are stated.
- [ ] At most 1 hashtag, at most 2 emoji.
- [ ] Any link/URL in the post either came directly from the backend/context or was built strictly from a real ROUTES.md pattern plus real values from a query — never hand-constructed from memory or guesswork (section 9).
- [ ] No leftover placeholder text (`{url}`, `[link]`, etc.) in the final post.
- [ ] If it names a customer, approval is on file.
- [ ] Read it out loud — does it sound like a person, not a press release?
